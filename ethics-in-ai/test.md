# Characteristics and Criteria of Profession in Nursing
## Understanding Professional Development in Healthcare

---

# Introduction to Professional Characteristics

Nursing has evolved from an occupation to an emerging profession, characterized by specific attributes that define professional practice.

Key Points:
- Historical evolution of nursing
- Current status as an emerging profession
- Importance of understanding professional characteristics

---

# General Characteristics of a Profession

1. Authority to control its own work
2. Exclusive body of specialized knowledge
3. Extensive period of formal training
4. Specialized competence
5. Control over work performance
6. Service to society
7. Self-regulation
8. Credentialing system
9. Legal reinforcement
10. Ethical practice
11. Collegial subculture
12. Intrinsic rewards
13. Public acceptance

---

# Wickenden's Six Characteristics

1. **Specialized Service**
   - Based on advanced knowledge and skill
   - Primarily intellectual rather than physical
   - Problem-solving oriented

2. **Confidential Relationship**
   - Between practitioner and client
   - Professional trust and privacy

3. **Public Obligation**
   - Responsibility due to specialized knowledge
   - Commitment to public welfare

---

# Wickenden's Characteristics (Continued)

4. **Common Heritage**
   - Shared knowledge and skills
   - Collective contribution to profession

5. **Public Interest Service**
   - Focus on general public benefit
   - Limited fees rather than direct profit

6. **Ethical Code**
   - Distinctive professional ethics
   - Guidelines for relationships with:
     - Clients
     - Colleagues
     - Public

# Bixler and Bixler Criteria (1945)

1. **Well-Defined Knowledge Base**
   - Organized body of knowledge
   - Higher intellectual level training

2. **Continuous Knowledge Enhancement**
   - Constant expansion of knowledge
   - Improved techniques through scientific method

3. **Higher Education**
   - Education through higher institutions
   - Professional preparation

4. **Practical Application**
   - Knowledge applied to vital human services
   - Focus on social welfare

5. **Professional Autonomy**
   - Independent policy formulation
   - Control of professional activities

6. **Service-Oriented Individuals**
   - Attracts intellectual individuals
   - Service valued above personal gain
   - Life-long commitment

7. **Professional Compensation**
   - Freedom of action
   - Continuous growth opportunities
   - Economic security

*Note: These criteria were significant in shaping nursing's professional development*

# Key Professional Statements

## Service and Society
> "The services provided are vital to humanity and the welfare of society"

- If 10 students are asked why they chose nursing, most would reply, "To help people"
- Essential to well-being of people and society
- Promotes health maintenance and restoration

---

# Knowledge and Research

> "There is a special body of knowledge that is continually enlarged through research"

- Evolution from borrowed principles to own research base
- Rapid expansion in last 20 years
- Shift from task orientation to research-based practice

---

# Intellectual Activities and Accountability

> "The services involve intellectual activities; individual responsibility (accountability) is a strong feature"

Key Features:
- Unique nursing process
- Critical and creative thinking
- Individual accountability as practice hallmark

---

# Professional Independence

> "Practitioners are relatively independent and control their own policies and activities (Autonomy)"

Current Challenges:
- Hospital hierarchy constraints
- Physician authorization requirements
- Multiple controlling groups:
  1. Organized medicine
  2. Health service administration
  3. Organized nursing

---

# Service Motivation

> "Practitioners are motivated by service (Altruism) and consider their work as an important component of their lives"

Key Points:
- Dedication to service
- Balance between altruism and economic needs
- Professional value recognition

---

# Ethical Framework

> "There is a code of ethics to guide the decisions and conduct of practitioners"

Standards:
- Professional framework for decision-making
- Public trust maintenance
- ICN and ANA established codes

---

# Professional Organization

> "There is an organization (association) that encourages and supports high standards of practice"

Current Status:
- Multiple professional associations
- ANA as primary advocate
- Challenge: Only 10% membership of 2.2 million nurses
1. **Service to Humanity**
   - Essential to society's well-being
   - Focuses on health maintenance and restoration
   - Promotes highest level of wellness

2. **Knowledge Base**
   - Evolved from borrowed principles to own research
   - No longer based on task orientation
   - Research-based practice development

3. **Professional Process**
   - Unique nursing process approach
   - Critical and creative thinking
   - Strong accountability framework

## Professional Challenges
1. **Educational Diversity**
   - Multiple entry levels to practice
   - Ongoing debate about minimum qualifications
   - Need for standardization

2. **Autonomy Issues**
   - Hospital hierarchy constraints
   - Physician dependency
   - Limited decision-making power

3. **Organizational Fragmentation**
   - Low professional membership (10% in ANA)
   - Multiple competing organizations
   - Reduced collective bargaining power
